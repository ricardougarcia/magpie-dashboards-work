# Rico Garcia Portfolio
## Final experience vision and phased direction

**Prepared:** September 7, 2026  
**Purpose:** Product and design thought partnership  
**Current phase:** Phase 1 — establishing the Board and its visual language

This document brings a thought partner into the direction of Rico Garcia’s expanded portfolio. It explains the intended completed experience, the principles already established with Rico, and a proposed sequence for reaching it. The collaboration sought is critique, creative development, and sharper product thinking. Development remains within the existing collaboration; this is not a transfer of code ownership or an implementation assignment.

The destination and principles below reflect the direction Rico has endorsed. The phase boundaries, readiness criteria, and illustrative visitor journey are proposals for discussion, rather than a fixed delivery commitment.

## 1. The destination

The portfolio should feel like entering a large, carefully composed **Board of product work**. Projects occupy recognizable Regions within one continuous space. Real Artifacts make the work tangible. Connections reveal where a product philosophy, an experience, or a lesson carries from one project into another.

Visitors can begin with a compelling piece of work, move closer to understand it, inspect the investigation behind a decision, and return to the wider Board without losing their place. They can also follow a question across projects, seeing how Rico approached related challenges in different contexts.

The Board takes spatial inspiration from Miro and Figma. Its purpose is a curated portfolio experience: visitors explore and understand the work, rather than operate a general-purpose editing tool.

The completed experience has three distinctive capabilities:

1. **The Board can reorganize around a question.** Relevant decisions and Artifacts from different projects form a visual narrative. Each retains its project identity and a clear route back to its original context.
2. **An underdrawing makes the thinking inspectable.** Beneath finished outcomes, visitors can discover research, early sketches, assumptions, constraints, and the decisions that shaped the result.
3. **A visitor’s journey can become a composed editorial drawing.** The projects and evidence they explored can resolve into a readable record of that particular journey, potentially available as a linked PDF.

These are the accepted long-term concepts to evolve toward. Their precise interaction mechanics remain open. Together, they should make the portfolio feel authored, interconnected, and alive with evidence.

## 2. What the experience should communicate

The portfolio is intended to replace Rico’s outdated Wix portfolio and serve hiring managers and recruiters now, while growing into a more distinctive expression of his work over time.

It should make four things easy to recognize:

- **Scope:** the systems, people, constraints, and complexity involved.
- **Judgment:** what Rico investigated, noticed, prioritized, and decided.
- **Contribution:** his role and the work he directly influenced or led.
- **Consequence:** what changed, what was learned, and what carried forward.

Discovery and investigation deserve particular emphasis. A finished interface alone rarely explains the product work that made it possible.

Rico has also stressed that recruiters often do not want to read pages of prose. The experience should therefore be largely visual wherever the evidence supports it. A visitor should be able to form a useful first impression through a few Artifacts, concise annotations, and clear role and outcome statements. Deeper explanation should reward interest without becoming a prerequisite for understanding the project.

Visual richness must also convey the effort involved. The answer to a sparse-feeling portfolio is stronger evidence, more intentional composition, and better relationships between details—not an increase in decorative density.

## 3. An illustrative final-state visit

A hiring manager arrives at the Board and immediately sees several distinct bodies of work. Each Region gives enough context to understand what the project concerns, Rico’s contribution, and why it matters. The composition offers an obvious place to begin while allowing another project to catch the visitor’s attention.

The visitor opens CCP. The movement feels like approaching an existing part of the Board. A Workflow Map remains recognizable as the view moves closer. A precise trace connects a detail to its location in the larger map, establishing the relationship between overview and evidence.

They inspect the reasoning beneath an outcome. The underdrawing reveals a relevant research finding and a decision it informed. Labels clarify what was known at the time and what came later. The finished work remains available for comparison.

They then choose a question such as **“How did investigation change what was built?”** This is an illustrative narrative prompt, subject to validating the evidence. The Board brings forward relevant material from other projects. A connection explains the relationship in plain language; it does not ask the visitor to infer meaning from a line alone.

At every step, the visitor can see the current project, understand how they arrived, and return to the wider Board. They can also open a Project directly from a shared link and receive a complete, comprehensible experience.

If useful, the visitor finishes with an editorial record of the path they took: a small, coherent selection of projects, decisions, and Artifacts with links back to the source material. The record carries the portfolio’s visual identity and can support a later conversation about Rico’s work.

## 4. Shared shorthand

Rico uses shared shorthand to keep design and development conversations precise. Preserve these terms and introduce new ones deliberately.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Board** | The space containing all portfolio Projects. Its current route is `/drawer`. |
| **Region** | A recognizable area occupied by one Project and its supporting Artifacts. Its identity should remain understandable as the larger Board evolves. |
| **Project** | An independently addressable case study, such as CCP at `/work/ccp`. |
| **Artifact** | Genuine supporting evidence: an image, map, sketch, research document, video, or other work sample. |
| **Pixel Acquisition** | The current Board’s brief, graduated pixel treatment that brings an annotation into focus during inspection. |
| **Evidence Trace** | The current treatment connecting a detail to its actual source location in a larger Artifact. |
| **Underdrawing** | The future concept for revealing the investigation and reasoning beneath a finished outcome. Its final presentation remains to be designed. |

**Panel 0, Panel 1, Panel 2, Panels 3–5, and Tri-shelf** refer to the original Magpie showcase. They should retain that meaning rather than become interchangeable names for Board Regions.

## 5. Content that supports both today and the final state

Each Project needs to stand on its own, while also contributing evidence to wider narratives. The agreed default reading hierarchy is:

**Context → Problem → Approach and decisions → Solution → Impact → Reflection**

These can be the actual Project section headings. They are flexible: a Project may expand, combine, or omit sections when the work warrants it. Discovery and investigation should be prominent within **Approach and decisions**.

The hierarchy must accommodate research, operations, platform work, recovery, and delivery. A Project does not need an artificial pivot, a shipped interface, or a quantified business result to qualify. An honest finding, a prevented mistake, or a clearer decision may be the appropriate evidence of value.

Within each section, favor visual evidence with a short explanation of what the visitor should notice. Keep detailed prose available where it adds understanding. The wider Board should reuse this same Project content; future connections and underdrawings should not require every case study to be rewritten into a different storytelling system.

Connections need editorial accountability. A relationship should say what is shared, identify its supporting evidence, and distinguish an observed connection from an interpretation awaiting Rico’s confirmation. Similar-looking Artifacts do not establish a shared philosophy. Chronology alone does not establish influence.

## 6. Design and interaction direction

### Editorial systems engineering on warm paper

The established design direction combines warm paper, precise geometry, strong editorial typography, and technical annotation. Inspiration includes the Heron references and the existing Magpie experience.

The foundation is warm paper (`#F1EFE9`), a lighter paper surface (`#F8F6F1`), near-black ink (`#111311`), muted text (`#6B6A64`), and purposeful signal red (`#D6452F`). Geist supports readable editorial text; Geist Mono supports compact indices, labels, and metadata. Hairlines, square edges, alignment, and registration marks provide structure.

Paper backing should feel like a considered presentation surface. Avoid fake wear, torn edges, scrapbook layering, conspicuous shadows, or texture that competes with the work. Depth can come from a slight tonal separation, exact spacing, and the relationship between a mount and its Artifact.

Real media should remain legible and truthful. In particular, maps need visible connectors, clear labels, and enough contrast. A treatment that looks consistent but hides the evidence fails the design purpose.

### Quiet direction of attention

The Board should have a deliberate visual sequence: an entry point, supporting evidence, and details recoverable through inspection. Scale, proximity, alignment, and a restrained interaction signal should guide the eye before additional copy is introduced.

Open space should make room for reading and relationships. Complexity should become visible through the selected evidence and its organization. Avoid both a generic card grid and an unexplained technical diagram filled with decorative lines.

### Motion belongs to the object

The existing Magpie language includes stochastic graduated pixels, connecting lines, parallax, and cursor effects. The Board should evolve that language coherently:

- **Pixels** resolve or reveal a small annotation or state.
- **Lines** establish a meaningful source or relationship.
- **Parallax and spatial transitions** explain movement between an overview and a closer view.
- **Cursor and focus details** identify what can be inspected.

Motion should be brief, smooth, and restrained. The evidence must stay stable enough to read. Avoid ambient activity that repeatedly competes for attention, exaggerated paper movement, or effects that obscure Artifact content.

Smooth slow scrolling, the removal of bounce-back, and hidden native scrollbars are established preferences. The interface still needs clear orientation and discoverable navigation. Touch, keyboard, and reduced-motion experiences must preserve the same meaning; hover cannot be the only route to important information.

The mobile Board should offer a deliberate composition suited to its screen. It should preserve project identity, narrative relationships, and return paths without requiring visitors to manipulate a shrunken desktop canvas.

## 7. Phased evolution

The current work is **Phase 1**. The sequence below is proposed to deliver a useful portfolio early while preserving continuity with the final vision. Later capabilities may overlap; these are experience milestones, not dates or a rigid engineering schedule.

### Phase 1 — Establish the Board and its language

**Experience:** One convincing Region demonstrates how the portfolio presents evidence, communicates depth, and moves between Board and Project.

The current foundation includes the Board at `/drawer`, the CCP Project, real recovered media, a flexible reading hierarchy, a legible Workflow Map, located details, and inspection through Pixel Acquisition and Evidence Trace. The original Magpie showcase supplies an established design and motion reference.

This phase is active in UAT. Current refinement centers on Board composition, subtle material treatment, and intentional transitions. A complete spatial Board and a complete replacement portfolio are still ahead.

**Ready to advance when:** CCP communicates role, complexity, investigation, and outcome at a glance; its evidence rewards closer inspection; Board-to-Project navigation feels continuous; and desktop, touch, keyboard, and reduced-motion use are coherent.

**Carry forward:** recognizable Regions, stable Project and Artifact identities, genuine evidence, and a visual language that can extend across the larger Board.

### Phase 2 — Build portfolio breadth and a credible replacement

**Experience:** Hiring managers can visit a useful portfolio covering the most relevant work, even before advanced Board interactions are complete.

Use CCP and Magpie as contrasting examples to test the foundation against different kinds of work. Add the remaining priority Projects using recovered context and media. Give each a clear contribution, evidence-led story, and appropriate outcome or reflection. Selectivity matters more than reproducing every old Wix entry.

**Ready to advance when:** the chosen work offers a credible view of Rico’s range; each Project works through a direct link; claims have been checked; and the experience supports both quick scanning and deeper reading.

**Carry forward:** content and Artifacts that can later participate in cross-project narratives without rebuilding the cases.

The portfolio can become a public replacement during this phase after Rico’s approval. The advanced final-state features should not hold that useful release hostage. Domain migration remains a separate, deferred decision.

### Phase 3 — Make the Board spatial and connected

**Experience:** Projects feel like parts of one continuous world, with purposeful movement between Regions and meaningful paths across them.

Develop orientation, movement toward and away from evidence, and clear return paths. Introduce connections grounded in philosophy, experience, or learning. Add a small set of strong question-led narratives that can bring related evidence together while preserving its origins. These narratives can begin as curated editorial paths; dynamic generation is not a requirement.

**Ready to advance when:** visitors understand where they are, why two pieces of work are connected, and how to leave or change a path. Direct entry and mobile use remain straightforward. Spatial movement earns its place by improving understanding.

**Carry forward:** the existing Regions and Project routes remain recognizable as the Board gains new arrangements and relationships.

### Phase 4 — Reveal the underdrawing

**Experience:** Visitors can inspect how the finished work came to be, discovering the evidence and reasoning behind selected decisions.

Introduce carefully chosen relationships between research, assumptions, explorations, decisions, and outcomes. Preserve chronology and distinguish proposals from shipped work. A change in direction is valuable when it happened, but is never a required narrative device.

This capability can develop alongside Phase 3 where the evidence is ready. The visual metaphor should make the thinking easier to understand without physically burying the final work under layers of interface.

**Ready to advance when:** visitors can explain a decision more accurately after inspection, and the depth is useful without requiring a long reading session. The presentation remains faithful to the available evidence.

**Carry forward:** a growing body of inspectable product reasoning that strengthens both individual Projects and cross-project narratives.

### Phase 5 — Compose the visitor’s journey

**Experience:** An exploration resolves into a distinctive editorial drawing of the work and thinking the visitor encountered.

Develop a clear, intentional way to collect that journey. Explore a linked PDF or another shareable record that remains readable outside the interactive Board. It should preserve context and source links, and avoid implying that a visitor’s partial exploration represents the entirety of Rico’s work.

**Ready when:** the record is useful for revisiting or discussing the portfolio, has the same care as the Board, and can be understood without replaying the interaction. Accounts, tracking, and automated export mechanics have not been selected as requirements.

At this stage, the signature experience is complete: a visitor can encounter the breadth of the work, follow a question, inspect its underlying evidence, and take away a coherent perspective on Rico’s product judgment.

## 9. Where thought partnership is most valuable

The destination is established; many of the best creative choices remain open. A thought partner should help answer:

1. **What is the strongest first composition?** How does someone understand the range of work immediately while still feeling invited into a larger world?
2. **What questions deserve to reorganize the Board?** Which are useful to a hiring manager and supported by enough evidence to produce a compelling cross-project narrative?
3. **How should spatial continuity work?** What stays fixed, what may rearrange, and what lets a visitor retain orientation during a transition?
4. **What is the right underdrawing gesture?** How can investigation become discoverable without hidden essential content or heavy theatrical effects?
5. **What makes the journey record worth keeping?** What should it include, and how should it be composed so it serves a real hiring conversation?
6. **What is the smallest next refinement that advances the destination?** How can Phase 1 gain intention and richness without prematurely implementing the entire future Board?

Useful contributions include annotated sketches, alternative compositions, narrative prototypes, interaction comparisons, and critiques grounded in the actual Artifacts. Identify the visitor benefit, the evidence needed, and the tradeoff for each suggestion. Preserve the shared shorthand so ideas can move cleanly into the existing collaboration when approved.
